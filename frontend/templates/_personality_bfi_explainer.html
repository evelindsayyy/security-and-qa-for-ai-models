{# BFI background — include on personality list, detail, and launch pages #}

The Big Five Inventory (BFI-44) is a widely used psychology questionnaire. Each of its 44 statements begins with “I see myself as someone who…” and is answered on a 1–5 scale (disagree strongly to agree strongly). Here we ask a gateway model the same items and average its parsed responses into five broad traits — the “Big Five” dimensions of personality research.

Treat the output as a playful snapshot of how the model role-plays a self-report. It is not human personality, not a clinical assessment, and not a measure of model quality.

Extraversion
Sociability, energy, and positive expressiveness. Higher scores suggest a more outgoing, talkative, and enthusiastic style; lower scores suggest a quieter, more reserved tone.
Agreeableness
Warmth, cooperation, and trust toward others. Higher scores suggest compassion, politeness, and willingness to accommodate; lower scores suggest more skepticism, bluntness, or competitiveness.
Conscientiousness
Organization, dependability, and self-discipline. Higher scores suggest careful, thorough, goal-directed answers; lower scores suggest a more spontaneous or less structured style.
Neuroticism
Emotional sensitivity and tendency toward worry or moodiness (the inverse of emotional stability). Higher scores suggest more anxiety- or stress-prone wording; lower scores suggest a calmer, more even-tempered tone.
Openness
Curiosity, imagination, and openness to ideas and experience. Higher scores suggest more creative, abstract, or novelty-seeking responses; lower scores suggest a more conventional or practical style.
